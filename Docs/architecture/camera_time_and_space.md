# Camera, time and space

Status: normative.

Geometry first resolves camera and screen transforms, their physical separation and the ideal camera rays required to determine optical-cover incidence and reflection direction. Cover and environment consume that typed geometry checkpoint in linear radiance. Lens character then applies focus, physical aperture integration, distortion, chromatic behavior, vignetting, transmission, softness and wide-field veiling glare to the covered signal. All three stages belong to the same Draft and Native optical request; none is a post-process route.

The canonical scene is right-handed. `+X` is right, `+Y` is up and `+Z` points from the screen toward a frontal viewer. A screen's local active area lies on XY, its center is the local origin and its front normal is `+Z`. A camera looks along local `-Z`.

Scene translation and physical screen dimensions use meters. Focal length and sensor aperture use millimeters. Persisted values always name their unit through the owning contract rather than relying on UI labels.

Project, source, camera and screen time use exact rational values. Project frame rate is an authored positive rational value and remains distinct from source cadence and panel refresh. A project frame maps to time by exact rational division; floating-point seconds are not an authored or media-selection boundary.

Camera and screen motion use the same current keyframed transform contract. Camera transform and camera intrinsics are independent tracks and are resolved at the requested rational time into one immutable sample. Translation is canonical in meters and rotation is canonical as a normalized quaternion. Free authoring exposes `Rotación X`, `Rotación Y` and `Rotación Z` in degrees, applied in that order; quaternion components, yaw/pitch/roll terminology and `W` are never user-facing controls. Look At is an editing projection for planar-screen work: distance plus orbital `Orientación X` and `Orientación Y` determine the camera position around the screen center, and the canonical quaternion is derived so the camera continues to point at that center. Switching Look At to Free materializes the same camera pose; switching Free to Look At derives distance and orbital orientation from the current relative position and retargets the screen center. These representations are editing projections, not persisted rotation authorities. Every keyframe has a globally unique id within its track, rational time, exact value and explicit `hold`, `linear`, or `smooth` interpolation. Interactive camera controls create one transient track derived from the committed track at the selected exact time and evaluate it through the same geometry path. `Set key` commits that derived state atomically; replacing a key preserves its stable ids. Changing timeline time cancels an uncommitted edit and repopulates the controls from the resolved committed sample.

The native Viewer additionally provides direct camera navigation over that same canonical pose. Middle-button Pan moves only camera translation along camera-local Right/Up with world distance derived from optical depth, physical FOV and the displayed image viewport. Option-middle-button Orbit locks to the first dominant drag axis and rotates camera position plus quaternion rigidly around the transformed Device active-area center, using the Device's transformed local Up or Right axis; it never edits the Device transform or recenters a prior Pan. Shift-middle-button and the wheel Dolly along camera-local Forward with multiplicative depth response and a near-plane bound evaluated against all four transformed Device corners. Starting any gesture materializes the current Look At pose as a transient Free pose without changing the visible projection. The transient pose updates only the lightweight Setup image during drag; mouse-up resolves it once through Free authoring, refreshes numeric controls, publishes full-resolution Setup and commits one undoable camera edit. Transient controller state is never persisted. Numeric camera or Device edits and Viewer navigation therefore remain bidirectional projections of one authored state rather than parallel cameras.

Reference matching is another editor of that same camera state. Application first inverts the authored Delivery Raster placement (`Fit`, `Fill / Crop` or `One to One`) so a reference-image pixel becomes an exact selected-camera gate pixel even when the two rasters have different aspect ratios. The Viewer separates four authored 2D targets from the four corners currently projected by the rigid Device: projected corners are red crosses, while four yellow targets are initialized with a fixed inset inside the visible reference raster even when the current Device projection is entirely offscreen. Shift-click fixes or releases a target and turns it green; once all four are fixed, a green quadrilateral joins them without changing their coordinates. A target never follows a camera solve or another target. It can be moved freely without changing camera state; only after all four targets are fixed does Application invert Brown-Conrady distortion and lens shift, solve one calibrated planar camera pose from the four 3D/2D correspondences and regenerate the red projection. `Resolver` uses the explicitly authored focal length. The selected lens template prepares an explicit focal interval from one half to twice its nominal focal length. `Buscar focal` evaluates that interval on a logarithmic coarse grid, refines the best neighboring interval by reprojection RMS, and commits the winning focal length and rigid camera pose atomically. The resulting RMS and maximum reprojection errors remain visible, so an inconsistent quadrilateral is reported rather than absorbed by deforming the Device. A solve commits one camera/intrinsics edit and one Undo operation; camera, focal length, target and projection are never updated as a coupled per-mouse-move corner pin. Physical camera navigation is unavailable during Match, while presentation-only Viewer pan, wheel/pinch zoom and Shift-MMB zoom remain available for subpixel placement. No homography is persisted and the Device is never deformed, moved or scaled. Degenerate targets, a pose behind the camera or a non-invertible lens projection fail explicitly. Automatic corner detection and tracking remain separate future capabilities.

Interpolation belongs to the outgoing keyframe. Quaternion rotation uses shortest-path SLERP; `smooth` applies its authored smoothstep timing before SLERP. Evaluation before the first keyframe holds the first authored value and evaluation after the last keyframe holds the last authored value; a one-key track is therefore explicitly static. Adding or replacing a keyframe in the desktop converts its distance, orbit-yaw and orbit-pitch editing projection immediately into canonical three-dimensional translation and quaternion values whose optical axis targets the panel origin. Playback consumes only the resulting track.

Camera intrinsics own focal length, sensor aperture, lens shift, focus distance, f-stop and clipping planes. Lens shift is dimensionless in sensor-gate widths/heights, is applied before distortion inversion and is limited to `[-0.5, 0.5]` per axis in the current contract. The resolver is the single owner of interpolation and emits one immutable camera sample containing row-major canonical world-to-view and ideal pre-distortion view-to-clip matrices plus resolved intrinsics. Canonical view space uses `+Z` forward and clip depth `[-1,+1]` from near to far; the raster/viewport adapter alone flips canonical `+Y` up to row coordinates increasing downward. Geometry consumes that sample and never reads animation tracks directly.

The current evaluator is a deterministic thin-lens camera. Focal length and f-stop derive the physical circular aperture. The modulation-free spatial evaluator integrates one globally selected nested 16, 32, 64, 128, 256 or 512-point equal-weight aperture pattern according to the worst estimated circle of confusion among panel center and all four corners. Its quality choice is constant for the complete output frame and every independently evaluated sensor region, so sampling density cannot create spatial seams, and every higher level preserves every ray from the lower levels. The 256/512-ray footprints are heap-backed so convergence cannot depend on worker-stack capacity or a reduced-quality fallback. The complete physical-frame evaluator uses exactly 32 direct equal-weight pupil rays at every quality and keeps that aperture axis independent from sensor-footprint and PSF samples. Every output pixel uses the same stable pupil orientation and ray order, so finite quadrature cannot turn smooth chromatic geometry into pixel-dependent color displacement. Every ray retains its per-channel focus distance and chromatic geometry, so resolved panel phase, RGB fringe and defocus remain coupled before the sensor. The natural-vignetting and pupil-throughput weight depends only on the resolved ideal sensor point and channel; it is prepared once for each sensor-footprint/PSF sample and reused unchanged across its direct pupil rays. All direct pupil rays converge on the authored plane perpendicular to the optical axis at focus distance. The photosite footprint is convolved through a centered, unit-normalized deterministic circular PSF quadrature consisting of the lens-authored center and edge softness in sensor-plane micrometers plus the green-wavelength Airy radius derived from f-number. Lens softness interpolates radially by squared normalized image height, and the complete uncapped physical radius is divided by the current photosite pitch only at evaluation. Draft and Native therefore represent the same physical blur at different sampling densities rather than sharing a presentation-space blur. That expansion applies in both unresolved area integration and resolved multisample evaluation; resolving the panel cannot bypass lens MTF. This is a photographic MTF approximation, not a spectral diffraction claim. Optical throughput contains the inverse-square f-number term exactly once in the lens result; the sensor boundary cannot apply it again. Near and far clipping are evaluated as optical-axis distances. The current evaluator requires the authored output aspect to equal the resolved sensor-aperture aspect; a future gate policy must be explicit rather than inferred.

The VFX 2D depth-of-field evaluator keeps the same chief-ray projection and replaces only the discrete pupil axis by a centered continuous rectangular footprint. In this evaluator alone, the authored PSF softness and Airy radius combine by root-sum-square variance matching so the fast VFX approximation does not over-broaden the in-focus micrometric core; the canonical direct-pupil thin-lens evaluator retains its calibrated additive PSF. The pupil footprint collapses at the channel's authored focus plane. Four additional chief rays project the sensor-pixel half-extent into panel UV per channel; camera-normalized extents cannot be added directly to a panel-normalized pupil footprint. The projected sensor extent and pupil extent add per axis as convolution support, matching the stronger visual focus falloff required by the VFX evaluator while retaining exact projected-sensor identity at zero pupil extent. To retain the perceptual fixed-pixel carrier without sharpening the content envelope, the VFX approximation samples the canonical device signal through one quarter of the projected horizontal sensor-pixel half-extent and the complete projected vertical sensor-pixel half-extent at the chief ray, then adds the complete per-channel pupil half-extent to both carrier axes. It subtracts the complete defocus-footprint panel result and adds only that carrier residual before Sensor/CFA; Panel Structure zero removes it exactly. The anisotropic in-focus core matches the owned vertical RGB-stripe topology: horizontal prefiltering suppresses the excessive low-frequency beats of three emitters per pixel while the complete vertical sensor footprint retains the black-matrix row coverage that a narrow phase sample can miss. As defocus grows, the same physical pupil footprint progressively suppresses the residual instead of leaving a sharp carrier over a defocused content envelope. The later Sensor/CFA remains the sole camera-sampling owner. Reusing the complete defocus-footprint averaged device code for the carrier sample is forbidden because that signal has already lost that phase. The complete footprint still owns content, cover, glow and defocus support. Both footprints remain centered on the chief ray and grow continuously away from focus before Sensor/CFA, so this is not a post-sensor blur or a second lens phase.

The authored lens approximation contains nominal focal length, Brown-Conrady radial and tangential distortion, per-emitter longitudinal focus offsets, per-emitter lateral magnification, natural cos-fourth vignetting strength, emitter transmission, center/edge sensor-plane softness and one bounded veiling-glare fraction. Veiling glare redistributes that fraction toward the covered panel's gate-average linear irradiance after the local cover response and before shutter integration. It is intentionally a low-cost infinite-support tail for VFX matching; it preserves exact identity at zero and does not claim field-dependent ghosts or a measured GSF. Geometry owns one catalog of complete lens authoring templates: generic photographic primes at 18, 25, 35, 50, 85 and 135 mm plus integrated/reference approximations for iPhone 16e, Canon A470 and iPhone 14 Pro main/ultra-wide validation cases. Reference presets authored against developed JPEG/HEIC imagery own only residual post-geometric-correction distortion; they cannot reapply an uncorrected/raw lens warp to an already corrected photographic reference. A future raw-calibrated preset is a distinct authored calibration. Integrated and interchangeable optics are the same lens-domain concept. A camera preset owns only a fixed/interchangeable association policy, one default lens id, its explicit compatible-lens ids and one explicit default Lens evaluation model; it never duplicates focal length or aberration coefficients. Selection validates compatibility and copies complete camera and lens values into camera intrinsics, and atomically restores the camera-owned evaluator default. Evaluation consumes the resolved evaluator literally and never derives it from camera identity, quality or output raster. Interpolation operates directly on the authored coefficients.

The accepted Brown-Conrady coefficient domain is explicitly bounded to the current catalog and modest custom/interpolated values. Bounds alone never certify a lens: bounded Newton inversion must additionally remain finite, convergent, round-trippable and orientation-preserving on a fixed 65×65 lattice covering the complete shifted sensor gate. Every resolved animated sample is revalidated, so an invalid interpolation fails rather than selecting another lens. Expanding the domain requires expanding its adversarial probes rather than weakening validation. The three physical panel emitters trace independent rays in the panel's native primary basis and convert to ACEScg only after optical integration. This is the current physically motivated tristimulus approximation; there is no post-process chromatic-aberration route.

Lens integration occurs in linear emitted radiance. Geometry converts luminance to image-plane illuminance through the explicit Lambertian `pi/4` pupil factor together with the inverse-square f-number term. Application then integrates shutter time and explicit optical ND in stops. The resulting ACEScg lux-seconds value is immutable before sensor response or any preview transform. The technical desktop owns an explicit, non-project preview exposure in EV only for optical and diagnostic views; changing it cannot alter geometry, focus, throughput, the linear optical result or persisted simulation state. Camera-result previews instead consume the authored capture exposure and output transform. Future still and OFX adapters must consume this same linear result.

Lens character is applied at the application boundary before projection. Zero produces an ideal lens
and also reduces the complete authored PSF, including diffraction, to zero; one preserves the lens
preset; values above one extrapolate distortion, chromatic behavior, vignetting, transmission,
PSF strength and veiling-glare fraction. This is a diagnostic/creative parameter on the current optical request, not a second
camera model.

A transient inspection camera may be framed from an explicit physical region of the transformed panel. It is resolved by the same geometry and projection path as the authored camera, preserves the current focal length, sensor, aperture and incidence angle, changes physical position to frame the region and explicitly refocuses that region. Whether subpixels are resolved is derived from the per-channel sensor-pixel and aperture footprint, not pinhole magnification alone. At deep oblique inspection, parts of the complete panel may lie behind the camera; the optional full-panel outline is then absent without invalidating the explicitly framed region. Inspection state is workstation interaction state and is not a persisted project camera.

The optical result remains irradiance at the virtual sensor plane and never changes when capture settings change. The current capture boundary integrates that result through one exact global/rolling-shutter contract. Authored motion samples resolve geometry and source time, while the separable residual-flicker and optional banding gain is integrated analytically over each sample interval before CFA photosites are recorded through `screen-sensor`. Demosaic, camera color processing and encoding remain later boundaries and do not alter lens or sensor evaluation.

The current version authors camera animation inside the application. External camera files do not have a runtime evaluation route.

Desktop undo history is interaction state, not project state. A pending transient camera edit is the first undo target. Each committed camera edit stores one complete prior camera rig, so undo and redo cannot split transform and intrinsics or expose a half-applied keyframe. A new commit clears redo. Undo history is never consulted by project opening, evaluation or persistence.

The desktop focus editor has one explicit `Autofocus` authoring policy and one resolved intrinsics value. With Autofocus enabled, Look At resolves the authored camera distance and Free resolves the positive optical-axis intersection with the transformed screen plane. A parallel optical axis, an intersection behind the camera or a result outside the focus domain fails explicitly. With Autofocus disabled, the independent manual focus-distance control is authoritative. Application resolves the policy before the physical request; the evaluator receives only the resulting explicit focus distance and has no autofocus fallback. Export metadata records both the authored policy and the resolved distance.

Diafragma and exposure time are authored physical values, not preview corrections. The selected camera preset supplies their reset values; editing either updates the immutable physical request used by interactive and reference evaluation. Exposure time is represented as the exact centered shutter interval at the host boundary before it enters the rational shutter contract. Test presents that single duration as shutter angle using the effective project frame rate and as synchronized reciprocal speed in `1/s`; changing either representation resolves the same duration. Character controls remain available in the card header even while its detailed controls are collapsed.
