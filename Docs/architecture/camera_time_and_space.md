# Camera, time and space

Status: normative.

Geometry carries optical-cover incidence and reflection direction on every ray in the same spatial/aperture/channel set used by lens integration. Application applies angle-dependent cover transmission and authored environment reflection in linear radiance inside that integration. This belongs to the shared Draft and Native optical request, not to a post-process route.

The canonical scene is right-handed. `+X` is right, `+Y` is up and `+Z` points from the screen toward a frontal viewer. A screen's local active area lies on XY, its center is the local origin and its front normal is `+Z`. A camera looks along local `-Z`.

Scene translation and physical screen dimensions use meters. Focal length and sensor aperture use millimeters. Persisted values always name their unit through the owning contract rather than relying on UI labels.

Project, source, camera and screen time use exact rational values. Project frame rate is an authored positive rational value and remains distinct from source cadence and panel refresh. A project frame maps to time by exact rational division; floating-point seconds are not an authored or media-selection boundary.

Camera and screen motion use the same current keyframed transform contract. Camera transform and camera intrinsics are independent tracks and are resolved at the requested rational time into one immutable sample. Translation is canonical in meters and rotation is canonical as a normalized quaternion. UI yaw, pitch and roll are editing projections, not persisted authority. Every keyframe has a globally unique id within its track, rational time, exact value and explicit `hold`, `linear`, or `smooth` interpolation. Interactive camera controls create one transient track derived from the committed track at the selected exact time and evaluate it through the same geometry path. `Set key` commits that derived state atomically; replacing a key preserves its stable ids. Changing timeline time cancels an uncommitted edit and repopulates the controls from the resolved committed sample.

Interpolation belongs to the outgoing keyframe. Quaternion rotation uses shortest-path SLERP; `smooth` applies its authored smoothstep timing before SLERP. Evaluation before the first keyframe holds the first authored value and evaluation after the last keyframe holds the last authored value; a one-key track is therefore explicitly static. Adding or replacing a keyframe in the desktop converts its distance, orbit-yaw and orbit-pitch editing projection immediately into canonical three-dimensional translation and quaternion values whose optical axis targets the panel origin. Playback consumes only the resulting track.

Camera intrinsics own focal length, sensor aperture, lens shift, focus distance, f-stop and clipping planes. Lens shift is dimensionless in sensor-gate widths/heights, is applied before distortion inversion and is limited to `[-0.5, 0.5]` per axis in the current contract. The resolver is the single owner of interpolation and emits one immutable camera sample containing row-major canonical world-to-view and ideal pre-distortion view-to-clip matrices plus resolved intrinsics. Canonical view space uses `+Z` forward and clip depth `[-1,+1]` from near to far; the raster/viewport adapter alone flips canonical `+Y` up to row coordinates increasing downward. Geometry consumes that sample and never reads animation tracks directly.

The current evaluator is a deterministic thin-lens camera. Focal length and f-stop derive the physical circular aperture; every output sample integrates one globally selected nested 16, 32, 64 or 128-point equal-weight aperture pattern according to the worst estimated circle of confusion among panel center and all four corners. The quality choice is constant for the complete output frame and every independently evaluated sensor region, so sampling density cannot create spatial seams, and every higher level preserves every ray from the lower levels. All rays for a sample converge on the authored plane perpendicular to the optical axis at focus distance. The photosite footprint is convolved through a deterministic circular PSF approximation consisting of the lens-authored center and edge softness in sensor-plane micrometers plus the green-wavelength Airy radius derived from f-number. Lens softness interpolates radially by squared normalized image height, and the complete uncapped physical radius is divided by the current photosite pitch only at evaluation. Draft and Native therefore represent the same physical blur at different sampling densities rather than sharing a presentation-space blur. That expansion applies in both unresolved area integration and resolved multisample evaluation; resolving the panel cannot bypass lens MTF. This is a photographic MTF approximation, not a spectral diffraction claim. Optical throughput contains the inverse-square f-number term. Near and far clipping are evaluated as optical-axis distances. The current evaluator requires the authored output aspect to equal the resolved sensor-aperture aspect; a future gate policy must be explicit rather than inferred.

The authored lens approximation contains Brown-Conrady radial and tangential distortion, per-emitter longitudinal focus offsets, per-emitter lateral magnification, natural cos-fourth vignetting strength, emitter transmission and center/edge sensor-plane softness. Geometry owns one catalog of complete lens authoring templates: generic photographic primes at 18, 25, 35, 50, 85 and 135 mm plus one calibrated approximation of the integrated iPhone 16e main lens. These are not commercial-lens claims. Selection copies complete values into camera intrinsics; evaluation never resolves a preset id, and interpolation operates directly on the authored coefficients.

The accepted Brown-Conrady coefficient domain is explicitly bounded to the current catalog and modest custom/interpolated values. Bounds alone never certify a lens: bounded Newton inversion must additionally remain finite, convergent, round-trippable and orientation-preserving on a fixed 65×65 lattice covering the complete shifted sensor gate. Every resolved animated sample is revalidated, so an invalid interpolation fails rather than selecting another lens. Expanding the domain requires expanding its adversarial probes rather than weakening validation. The three physical panel emitters trace independent rays in the panel's native primary basis and convert to ACEScg only after optical integration. This is the current physically motivated tristimulus approximation; there is no post-process chromatic-aberration route.

Lens integration occurs in linear emitted radiance. Geometry converts luminance to image-plane illuminance through the explicit Lambertian `pi/4` pupil factor together with the inverse-square f-number term. Application then integrates shutter time and explicit optical ND in stops. The resulting ACEScg lux-seconds value is immutable before sensor response or any preview transform. The technical desktop owns an explicit, non-project preview exposure in EV only for optical and diagnostic views; changing it cannot alter geometry, focus, throughput, the linear optical result or persisted simulation state. Camera-result previews instead consume the authored capture exposure and output transform. Future still and OFX adapters must consume this same linear result.

Lens character is applied at the application boundary before projection. Zero produces an ideal lens
and also reduces the complete authored PSF, including diffraction, to zero; one preserves the lens
preset; values above one extrapolate distortion, chromatic behavior, vignetting, transmission and
PSF strength. This is a diagnostic/creative parameter on the current optical request, not a second
camera model.

A transient inspection camera may be framed from an explicit physical region of the transformed panel. It is resolved by the same geometry and projection path as the authored camera, preserves the current focal length, sensor, aperture and incidence angle, changes physical position to frame the region and explicitly refocuses that region. Whether subpixels are resolved is derived from the per-channel sensor-pixel and aperture footprint, not pinhole magnification alone. At deep oblique inspection, parts of the complete panel may lie behind the camera; the optional full-panel outline is then absent without invalidating the explicitly framed region. Inspection state is workstation interaction state and is not a persisted project camera.

The optical result remains irradiance at the virtual sensor plane and never changes when capture settings change. The current capture boundary integrates that result through one exact global/rolling-shutter contract. Authored motion samples resolve geometry and source time, while the separable residual-flicker and optional banding gain is integrated analytically over each sample interval before CFA photosites are recorded through `screen-sensor`. Demosaic, camera color processing and encoding remain later boundaries and do not alter lens or sensor evaluation.

The current version authors camera animation inside the application. External camera files do not have a runtime evaluation route.

Desktop undo history is interaction state, not project state. A pending transient camera edit is the first undo target. Each committed camera edit stores one complete prior camera rig, so undo and redo cannot split transform and intrinsics or expose a half-applied keyframe. A new commit clears redo. Undo history is never consulted by project opening, evaluation or persistence.

The desktop focus editor has two explicit projections onto the same authored intrinsics value. `Panel` writes the current orbit distance as focus distance because the orbit optical axis targets the panel origin; `Manual` writes the independent focus-distance control. The evaluator receives only the resulting explicit focus distance and has no autofocus fallback.
